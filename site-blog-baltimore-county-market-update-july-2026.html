Baltimore County Market Update: This Month's Numbers

Baltimore County closed June with a median sold price of $399,000, holding steady against May and up modestly from a year ago. Homes are moving in a median of 24 days, and with 2.3 months of supply on hand, the county remains a seller-favoring market — though it's noticeably more balanced than the sub-two-week, bidding-war conditions of a couple years back. Statewide, Maryland's median sold price came in at $465,000, up 3.3% year-over-year according to Maryland REALTORS®.

What Rates Are Doing

The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate has been sitting in the high-6% range through late July 2026 — roughly 6.6–6.8% depending on the lender and your credit profile, per Bankrate and Freddie Mac survey data. That's meaningfully lower than the 7%+ rates buyers saw for stretches of the last few years, and it's part of why showings have picked back up this summer even as prices held firm.

What This Means If You're Selling

  • 24 median days on market means well-priced, well-presented homes are still moving fast — but pricing mistakes get punished quicker than they did in the frenzy years.
  • 2.3 months of supply is balanced-to-tight, not a landslide seller's market. Overpricing "to test it" costs you real time on market now.
  • Buyers who paused at 7%+ rates are re-entering the market at today's high-6s — more competition for your listing than there was in early spring.

What This Means If You're Buying

  • Rates in the high-6s won't feel great, but they're better than most of the last two years — and refinancing later if rates drop further is always an option.
  • 2.3 months of supply means more choices than the tightest markets of recent memory, especially in Reisterstown, Owings Mills, and Pikesville, where new listings have been a bit more plentiful this summer.
  • Pre-approval matters more than ever at 24 median days on market — homes priced right aren't waiting around.
