Pikesville, MD Homes for Sale & Open Houses: A Local Guide

Where Pikesville is, how to get around, what's nearby, what the housing looks like, and how to find open houses and homes for sale.

By Jerry Lyons, REALTOR® with Next Step Realty · November 2026

Pikesville sits just outside the Baltimore City line, right where Reisterstown Road meets the Beltway. It's an established area with a long main street of shops and services, an Olmsted-planned historic neighborhood, and a big redevelopment project at the old Armory. Here's a plain guide to where it is, how to get around, what's nearby, and how to see homes, including upcoming open houses.

Where Pikesville Is

Pikesville is in Baltimore County, northwest of Baltimore City. Baltimore County has no incorporated cities or towns, so Pikesville has no town government of its own. County government handles zoning, roads, parks, and libraries, and the U.S. Census Bureau tracks it as a census-designated place.

That matters when you shop. A "Pikesville" mailing address (ZIP code 21208) doesn't follow one official town line, so confirm the exact location, tax details, and school assignment for each address you're considering.

Getting Around

  • I-695 (the Baltimore Beltway) runs through Pikesville with several nearby exits: Exit 19 (I-795), Exit 20 (Reisterstown Road), Exit 21 (Park Heights Avenue / Stevenson Road), and Exit 22 (Greenspring Avenue).
  • Reisterstown Road (MD 140) is the main commercial road. Baltimore County's Pikesville Commercial Revitalization District runs along it between the Beltway and the Baltimore City line.
  • Metro: MDOT MTA's Metro SubwayLink has its Old Court and Milford Mill stations nearby, with trains toward downtown Baltimore and Johns Hopkins one way and Owings Mills the other. Schedules change, so check MDOT MTA before you count on a train commute.

As with any area, drive or ride your real commute on a weekday at the time you'd actually travel.

Landmarks and Everyday Places

  • Pikesville Armory: built in 1903 on a 14-acre site and used as a National Guard campus until 2016. The Maryland Department of Housing and Community Development reported in January 2025 that the redevelopment received $15 million in state revitalization tax credits, with planned public green space, recreation, art space, small-business space, veterans' services, and future housing, and completion projected for 2028.
  • Sudbrook Park Historic District: listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1973. The Maryland Historical Trust notes the curving streets of its Olmsted plan.
  • Pikesville Branch, Baltimore County Public Library (1301 Reisterstown Road).

Hours, facilities, and project timelines change, so check each official site before you count on them.

What the Housing Looks Like

The Census Bureau's American Community Survey (2020–2024 estimates) counts about 14,400 homes in the Pikesville census area:

  • Close to two in five are detached single-family homes.
  • About one in six are attached single-family homes, such as townhouses.
  • A little under half are in buildings with two or more units, from small condo buildings to larger apartment and condo complexes.

It's an established housing market. About one in five homes was built before 1960, and about one in four was built in 1990 or later, so most of the housing dates from the 1960s through the 1980s. Those counts include rentals as well as owner-occupied homes. In practice, a search here turns up older homes in historic areas like Sudbrook Park, mid-century single-family streets, townhomes, and condos.

With older homes, budget time for a thorough home inspection and ask about the age of the roof, HVAC, electrical panel, and sewer line. On a condo, read the condo documents and fees before you commit.

How to Find Open Houses in Pikesville

  • My open houses: the Open Houses page lists the public open houses I'm hosting and updates as soon as new ones are scheduled.
  • Pikesville listings: search Pikesville homes for sale through Next Step Realty. Open house times show on the listings that have them.
  • Confirm before you go. Open houses get canceled when a home goes under contract, so check the listing the morning of.

Two things to know when you walk in. In Maryland, the agent hosting an open house can meet the state's agency disclosure rule by posting a notice that they represent the seller, so assume they work for the seller. And you don't need to sign a buyer agreement just to talk with an agent at an open house. If you want an agent to take you on a private tour, a written buyer agreement comes first.

Schools

Pikesville is served by Baltimore County Public Schools. I don't rate or rank schools. Check boundaries and school information for a specific address at bcps.org.
