How to Pick the Right Maryland Suburb for You

A framework for comparing commute times, amenities, and price per square foot between any two Baltimore County neighborhoods.

By Jerry Lyons, REALTOR® with Next Step Realty · October 2026

Picking a suburb is part numbers, part feel. The trick is to compare places on the same terms, so you're not choosing based on one nice Saturday drive. Here's the framework I walk buyers through, using Baltimore County towns as examples. It works for any two neighborhoods in Maryland.

Step 1: Write Down What Has to Work

Before you look at a single listing, list your non-negotiables. Keep it short and specific:

  • Where you need to get to on a normal weekday, and by when
  • The type of home you want: single-family, townhome, or condo
  • Your real monthly budget, not just the pre-approval number
  • The everyday places you use most: grocery, gym, parks, trails, medical offices
  • Space needs: bedrooms, a yard, a garage, room to work from home

Then rank them. If two towns both check every box, the ranking breaks the tie.

Step 2: Test the Commute for Real

A map app on a Sunday afternoon will lie to you. Drive or ride the route at the time you'd actually travel, on a weekday, in both directions. Here's what the main routes look like from a few of the towns I work in:

  • Reisterstown and Owings Mills: I-795 connects to the Baltimore Beltway (I-695), and Reisterstown Road runs toward the city. The Metro SubwayLink starts at Owings Mills, with Baltimore County stops at Old Court and Milford Mill on the way to downtown and Johns Hopkins Hospital.
  • Pikesville: sits along Reisterstown Road with quick access to I-695, and is a short drive from the Old Court and Milford Mill subway stations.
  • Timonium: I-83 runs north toward Pennsylvania and south into the city. The Light RailLink stops at Timonium Fairgrounds and continues north to Hunt Valley and south through downtown toward BWI Airport.

Check MTA's current schedules and service alerts before you count on transit, and if you'll commute by car, time the trip during the busiest part of your day.

But a median hides a lot. If one town sold mostly townhomes last quarter and another sold mostly single-family homes on bigger lots, the medians aren't comparing the same thing.

That's where price per square foot helps. To use it fairly:

  • Compare the same property type: townhomes to townhomes, single-family to single-family.
  • Use closed sales, not asking prices, from the same time period.
  • Keep similar sizes together. Smaller homes often sell for more per square foot than larger ones.
  • Note age, condition, and lot size. A dated house on a big lot and a renovated one on a small lot can land at the same price for very different reasons.

It also helps to look at what's actually for sale. Owings Mills has newer subdivisions alongside established neighborhoods. Reisterstown mixes classic single-family homes with newer construction. Timonium has established single-family streets and townhome communities. If the home type you want rarely comes up in a town, that tells you something before you fall for it.

The table mixes every home type, so treat it as a starting point. I can pull closed-sale price per square foot from Bright MLS for any two neighborhoods you're weighing, filtered to the home type you actually want.

Step 4: Check the Everyday Stuff

This is the part people skip and later wish they hadn't. Visit each area at least three times:

  • A weekday morning, to see traffic and noise
  • A weekday evening, to see parking and activity
  • A weekend, to see the parks, shops, and restaurants you'd actually use

Walk the street you'd live on, not just the main road. Find your nearest grocery store, library branch, and park, and see how long it really takes to get there.

Step 5: Look Up the Official Details

Some answers should come straight from the source:

  • Schools: I don't rate or rank schools. For school assignments and information, go directly to Baltimore County Public Schools or Baltimore City Public Schools, and confirm the assignment for the specific address.
  • Property taxes: look up the home's assessment on the Maryland SDAT Real Property search. Rates differ by county and by town.
  • Flood zones: check the address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center.
  • Transit: routes and schedules at MDOT MTA.

Step 6: Score It Side by Side

Put your top two or three areas in a simple table. Rate each factor 1 to 5, and weight the ones that matter most to you:

  • Commute at your actual travel time
  • Homes of your type within budget
  • Price per square foot for that home type
  • Everyday amenities you'll use
  • Property taxes and any HOA fees
  • How it felt on your three visits

The highest score isn't automatically the winner, but it usually makes the decision a lot clearer. If you're moving from out of the area, my relocation guide covers the same ground with a live video tour on request.
